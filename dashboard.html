What we measured—and what we didn’t

An interpretable descriptive analysis, with a reproducible Python pipeline and explicit missing-data handling.

Data & sample

Source: the public dataset linked in the NYU event brief. Tracking is sampled at 10 Hz; player roles and coverage labels come from PFF.

Time to throw

Elapsed time is (tagged pass_forward frame − tagged ball_snap frame) ÷ 10. We require exactly one eligible snap and release, and a positive duration no greater than 12 seconds. Automatic markers are not substituted silently. The Schultz example has a 0.2-second difference between automatic and tagged release annotations.

Short pass ≠ short route

“Short” and “deep” come from explicit pass-description labels. We do not infer pass depth from total play yards, classify route concepts, or call a delayed short pass a checkdown. All release-time charts in this version use short-labeled attempts.

Targets & separation

We match a play description’s abbreviated target name to a unique WR/TE/RB present on that play, allowing different initial formats and compound surnames. Unmatched or ambiguous targets remain missing. A skill player initially assigned to block can still be the named target. Separation is Euclidean distance to the nearest of all 11 defenders at the tagged release, not an assigned matchup or catch probability.

Coordinate & event checks

The offense is normalized to move upfield. The line of scrimmage comes from play metadata: the ball may already have moved toward the QB in the tagged snap frame. The three inspected plays have 11 offensive players, 11 defenders, and one ball in every observed frame, with no snap-to-release gaps.

Limits on interpretation

Only thrown attempts are included; sacks and scrambles have no observed release time here. Short/deep labels are coarse, target matching can miss players, annotations have uncertainty, and groups differ in formation, route responsibility, down, opponent, and QB. We cannot infer intended reads, safe throwing lanes, missed throws, or causal effects.
